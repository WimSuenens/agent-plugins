# Design brief

Apply the principles below to a new, unrelated project. Do not reuse any
specific colours, fonts, or copy from a reference design — invent your own
palette, typography and words from these rules.

## Concept

Luxury argued through restraint rather than richness: hold the entire
palette to three colours — a warm/neutral paper ground, one deep ink for
grounding text and dark sections, and exactly one precious accent spent
sparingly. Structure the page as chapters that alternate between the light
ground and the dark ink ground, so the switch itself marks a change of
register (opening/closing statements on the dark ground, everything
persuasive in between on the light one).

## Colour strategy

Limit yourself to three colours, full stop — a ground, an ink, and one
accent — and resist adding a fourth "neutral" just to distinguish a new
kind of card. Build every card from the *ground colour itself* plus a
border and a shadow, never a tinted fill; a surface that needs to feel
distinct earns a border treatment, not a new swatch. Spend the one accent
on the smallest number of elements that will actually bias a decision (one
filled button on the page, say) rather than scattering it as a general
highlight colour.

## Type strategy

Carry every heading, numeral and label on one high-contrast display face,
and everything else on one plain, quiet companion face — the two should
never compete for the same job. Define exactly three tracking values and
lock each to one role only: a tight value for every headline, a
noticeably wider value for eyebrows, and a third, even wider value
reserved solely for your wordmark or single most identity-bearing piece of
text — never reuse the wordmark's tracking anywhere else, or it stops
reading as a signature.

## Shape & elevation

Build a card as an object, not a panel: same surface colour as the page
around it, a solid border in a colour one or two steps warmer/cooler than
the ground, and a paired shadow — a soft, coloured drop below plus a
crisp, light hairline highlight inset along the top edge. Both halves of
that pairing are required; drop either one and the card reads as a flat
screenshot rather than a lacquered object sitting on the page. Assign
radius per *role* (shells, cards, controls, pills each get their own
value) rather than per size, so a future theme can flatten one role
without flattening all of them.

## Motion

Give the page exactly one signature entrance device — headline text
revealed word by word from behind a moving mask — and use it to open every
section once, never mid-section and never a second time within the same
section. Everything else fades and rises once on scroll. Beyond that,
commit to stillness: no ambient loops, no pulsing, no marquee. That
absence of ambient motion is itself the design decision, not an oversight
— state it as one if you're documenting the choice.

## Voice

Number your section labels starting from the second section, and exempt
the opening one — it has no "before" to count from. Write headlines as two
short clauses split across exactly two lines, so a visual beat lands
between them. Replace generic software vocabulary with vocabulary native
to the domain you've actually chosen (a concierge, not a dashboard; a
journey, not a session) even when the underlying UI is, structurally,
exactly that generic thing. State every quantity precisely and never round
it. Keep reassurance lines to well under ten words each.

## Apply it

Take a brief for a completely different product or audience and design it
from these rules: your own three-colour palette and alternating-ground
chapters, your own border-plus-paired-shadow card device, your own two-
face type system with three locked tracking roles, your own single
word-reveal entrance, and your own domain-native voice.
