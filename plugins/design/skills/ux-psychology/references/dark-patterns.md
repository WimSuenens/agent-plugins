# Dark patterns: names and anchors

Reached from the three-check test when a finding needs the named pattern or its regulatory anchor. Name a pattern with the term from the first table so findings across reviews stay comparable.

## Which check catches which pattern

| Check failed | High-level strategy (Gray et al. 2024 ontology) | Named patterns | Regulatory anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Truthful framing | Sneaking, social engineering | Fake urgency / countdown, fake scarcity, fake social proof, fake reviews, fake reference price, hidden costs / drip pricing, bait and switch, disguised ads, fabricated progress | FTC 2022 cat. 1 (induce false beliefs) and 2 (hide or delay material information); EU UCPD misleading actions |
| 2. Symmetric exit | Obstruction, interface interference, forced action | Roach motel / hard to cancel, confirmshaming, visual interference (prominence asymmetry), nagging, preselection, forced registration, trick questions | DSA Art. 25(3)(a) more prominence to certain choices, (b) repeatedly requesting a choice already made, (c) termination harder than subscribing; FTC cat. 4 (privacy choices); GDPR: pre-ticked consent is not consent (CJEU *Planet49*, 2019) |
| 3. Endorsement | Remainder: true and symmetric but working against the user | Compulsion loops (variable reward without stopping cues), defaults set for margin rather than fit, forced continuity, unauthorized charges | DSA Art. 25(1) "materially distorts or impairs … free and informed decisions"; FTC cat. 3 (unauthorized charges) |

## Honest use vs manipulative form

| Principle | Honest use | Manipulative form | Fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defaults | What most would choose; easy to change | Pre-ticked add-ons or consent | 2, 3 |
| Choice overload | Curate, recommend, filter | Overwhelm toward the margin option; comparison prevention | 3 |
| Anchoring | Real reference points | Fake "was" prices; inflated presets; relative framing hiding recurring cost | 1 |
| Decoy | Clear, differentiated tiers | Phantom tiers | 3 |
| Framing | Accurate gain or loss | Confirmshaming; double negatives | 1, 2 |
| Loss aversion | Real losses, stated | Invented losses; resetting timers | 1 |
| Status quo | Respect familiar settings | Roach motel | 2 |
| Prompts (B = MAP) | Timely; respects "no" | Nagging | 2 |
| Goal gradient | Credit real steps | Fabricated progress; streak anxiety | 1, 3 |
| Variable reward | Novelty with stopping cues | Infinite scroll; paid random rewards; notification bait | 3 |
| IKEA effect | Setup the user keeps | Work held hostage behind an undisclosed wall | 1, 3 |
| Endowment | Full trial, clear end | Forced continuity | 1, 3 |
| Commitment | Small useful first step | Low-ball / bait-and-switch | 1 |
| Reciprocity | Unconditional value first | "Gift" gated after effort | 1 |
| Social proof | Real, verifiable counts | Fake activity, fake reviews | 1 |
| Aesthetics / credibility | Polish matching real quality | Fake trust badges; disguised ads | 1 |

## Why it matters commercially

Dark patterns work: in a controlled experiment, mild ones roughly doubled sign-ups and aggressive ones roughly quadrupled them, with little backlash for the mild ones (Luguri & Strahilevitz 2021). Hiding fees until checkout raised spending in a large field experiment (Blake et al. 2021). That effectiveness is exactly why regulators target them, so an honest alternative in a finding should name what it may cost short-term, not pretend it costs nothing.
